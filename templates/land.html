Two lots can sit side by side, look the same, and be worth very different amounts. A lot is worth what it can support: soil that passes a perc test, a well site or county water, a slope that takes a foundation and a driveway without heavy grading, road frontage, and zoning or covenants that allow what you want to build.

Karen helps buyers judge buildability and estimate site costs before the contract is firm. On Lake Lanier she confirms dock eligibility and shoreline buffer rules with the Corps of Engineers, since those decide whether a lakefront lot works the way a buyer expects.

We also sell land. Family acreage, a lake lot, a mountain tract or a parcel that can be subdivided: we market it to builders, investors and end buyers with the survey, soil data and site information they ask for first.

Questions land buyers ask.

Do I need a perc test?

Where there is no sewer, which is most of North Georgia outside city limits, yes. The test measures how fast the soil absorbs water. It decides whether the lot can have a septic system and how large a home that system can serve. We write it into the contract as a contingency.

How do I know a Lake Lanier lot can have a dock?

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers issues dock permits, and a permit belongs to the property. Karen confirms the permit status, the dock size allowed and the shoreline buffer rules with the Corps before a client commits.

How much does a steep lot add to the cost of building?

It can add six figures. Steep sites need engineered foundations, retaining walls, longer driveways and more grading. Karen helps you estimate the cost of the site before you pay for the view.

Can you help me find a builder?

Yes. We introduce land buyers to reputable local builders, review the construction contract and stay involved through the build.
