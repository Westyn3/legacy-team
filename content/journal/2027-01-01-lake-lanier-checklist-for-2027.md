A new year is a sensible time to sort out the details that slow a lake purchase or sale. None of this depends on where prices go. It is the groundwork I ask clients to put in place first, so that when the right property or the right buyer appears, nothing is left to chase.

## If you plan to buy

- **Decide how you will use the water.** Boating, swimming off the dock and a porch with a view each point to a different part of the lake. That answer tells me where to start.
- **Set your practical limits.** Name the drive you can live with, your school needs, and whether you want a club or marina community or a private stretch of shoreline.
- **Speak with your lender early.** A settled budget lets us move quickly when a lakefront home comes up.
- **Ask about depth at two levels.** On any property you take seriously, find out how deep the water is when the lake is full and how deep it stays when the level falls later in the year.
- **Plan to verify the dock.** Every lakefront home needs its Corps of Engineers permit status and allowed dock size confirmed before you commit.

Our [buyer page](/buy/) explains how we run a search from first call to closing.

## If you are considering a lot

A vacant lakefront lot needs its own checks. The shoreline buffer rules, dock eligibility, a perc test for septic and the slope of the land together decide what the lot can support. I write the perc test in as a contingency, and I estimate site costs before a client commits, because a steep lot can carry six figures of work before construction begins. The [land and lots](/land-and-lots/) page goes further.

## If you plan to sell

- **Locate your dock permit.** It belongs to the property, so a buyer will want to see exactly what it allows.
- **Collect your records.** Gather paperwork for the well, the septic system, the pool and any outbuildings.
- **Keep photos of your dock across the year.** Depth is one of the first questions a lake buyer raises, and pictures from different months help answer it.
- **Ask for a valuation built on true lakefront comparables.** A lake home priced against the county average is priced wrong.
- **Begin preparation early.** A walk-through in January leaves time to finish repairs and staging at an unhurried pace before the photographer comes.
- **Book the photography with the water in mind.** On the lake, aerial images and video of the dock and the shoreline do much of the selling.
