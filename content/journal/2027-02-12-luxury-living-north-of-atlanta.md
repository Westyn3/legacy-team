North of Atlanta there is no single luxury market. Between Buckhead and Blue Ridge there are at least five different ways to live well, each with its own homes, rhythm and trade-offs. Between the two of us, we work all of them, so here is the tour we give clients when they are not yet sure which one fits.

## The city estate

In [Buckhead](/buckhead/), the estate streets of Tuxedo Park and West Paces Ferry hold some of the most significant homes in Atlanta. Historic Brookhaven, set around the Capital City Club, offers a similar sense of permanence a few minutes away. This is luxury with the city at hand: restaurants, the Atlanta History Center, Chastain Park and private schools close by.

Britney leads our work here, and she is usually the one who knows which street or building fits which client.

## The gated golf community

Up the GA-400 corridor, [Alpharetta](/alpharetta/) and Cumming offer gated and golf communities with a club at the center of daily life. Windward in Alpharetta and the established communities of South Forsyth suit families who want security, amenities and a short drive to work. The house matters, and the club and the neighbors matter nearly as much.

## The estate on acreage

[Milton](/milton/) is where North Atlanta buyers come for land. Large-lot zoning protects its horse farms and wooded roads. The Manor and White Columns offer gated golf living, and the equestrian acreage along Birmingham Highway offers barns, pastures and room to ride. Here luxury means space and quiet more than anything else.

## The deep-water estate

On [Lake Lanier](/lake-lanier/), luxury is measured in water. A deep-water estate with its own dock changes how a family spends every summer weekend, and the communities on the Gainesville side add marinas and club life. Karen has lived on the lake for more than twelve years and leads this part of our work.

## The mountain retreat

Farther north, long-range-view homes in Blue Ridge, Achasta in [Dahlonega](/dahlonega/) and Big Canoe offer a different kind of luxury: cooler air, national forest and a town to walk into for dinner. Many owners use a mountain home part-time and rent it the rest of the year.

## How to choose

We start with three questions. How do you want to spend an ordinary Tuesday? How do you want to spend a Saturday? Who will visit, and how often? A client who wants to walk to dinner and a client who wants to ride every morning should not be shown the same houses, even at the same price.

Many families end up wanting two of these: a primary home near work and a lake or mountain place for weekends. That is one reason we work as a team. One of us knows the city and the corridor, the other knows the water and the mountains, and both of us keep track of your search.
