Once the holiday guests have gone home, the last days of December tend to be quiet. For an owner planning to sell a high-end home in the new year, that quiet is useful. A luxury sale depends on preparation that takes weeks, and very little of it requires the house to be on the market. Here is how we suggest using the time, for an estate in Milton, a house on the lake or a home in the mountains.

## Begin with the number

A luxury home should be priced against true comparables. An estate is measured against other estates, and a lake home against other lakefront, because a county average says almost nothing at this level. That analysis takes time, especially for a property with few close matches. We can prepare a valuation now with no obligation. It gives you a realistic figure to plan around, and it shows which features buyers in your price range will weigh most closely.

## Give the work time to be done well

The second step is a tour of the house together, followed by a written plan. It sets out what to put away, what to stage, and which repairs are likely to pay for themselves. Some of those items need a contractor, and the good ones are often booked weeks out. Karen spent years in construction management, and she can separate the items a buyer's inspector will flag from the ones that are cosmetic. On an estate with a pool, a guest house or a barn, that review covers every structure on the property.

## Put the paperwork in one folder

Specialty property sells on information, and high-end buyers ask for it early. A survey belongs in the folder for nearly every property. So do records for the well and septic system where the home has them, and permits for pools and outbuildings. On [Lake Lanier](/lake-lanier/), buyers will ask to see the dock permit and what size of dock it allows. For acreage or a [lot](/land-and-lots/), soil data and site details matter most. For a mountain home, gather any rental history. Each of these answers a question before it slows a contract.

## Choose the right moment for the photographs

Architectural and drone photography and cinematic video come with every listing we take, and the timing of the shoot deserves thought. Where the grounds carry much of the value, it can be worth waiting for green lawns and full trees. On the lake, the water level moves with the seasons, so a dock photographed at typical winter levels shows a different picture from one at full pool. In the mountains, winter can open a view that summer narrows, and buyers will ask whether it holds year-round. We plan the shoot around what the property needs to show, and staging guidance comes before the photographer arrives.

## Decide how public the sale should be

Some sellers prefer discreet marketing to a known circle of buyers and agents before a public listing, or in place of one. Others want the widest reach through our luxury and relocation contacts, the Keller Williams referral network and the major portals. Each path has trade-offs. The choice is easier to make calmly now than in the week a listing goes live.

None of this commits you to a date. It means that when you decide to list, the pricing is done, the house is ready and the documents are in hand.
