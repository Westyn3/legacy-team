I have lived on Lake Lanier for more than twelve years. Most buyers first see the lake on a summer afternoon, which is a fine introduction and a poor basis for a decision. A lake home has to work in January as well as July, so here is how I think about the full year.

## Winter shows you the truth

The lake level moves with the seasons and drops further in a dry year. In winter you see the shoreline as it really is: how far the water pulls back from the dock, whether the cove still holds usable depth and how steep the walk down has become. If I could choose when a buyer first saw a lakefront home, I would choose a gray afternoon in January.

Winter is also when the lake is quietest. Owners who live here full-time often tell me it is their favorite season, with long views through bare trees and the water nearly to themselves.

## Spring is for getting the house ready

By spring, owners are servicing boats, checking docks and clearing the slope to the water. For a buyer, it is a good time to see how a property handles rain: where water runs, whether the drive stays firm and how the shoreline holds up. For a seller, it is the season to have everything working before buyers arrive.

## Summer is the lake most people picture

In summer the lake is busy, especially on the south end near [Buford](/buford/) and Lake Lanier Islands. That energy is exactly what some families want: friends on the dock, a boat ride to dinner and grandchildren in the water all afternoon. Others prefer the quieter, deeper water on the north end near [Dawsonville](/dawsonville/) and the Cumming shores. Spend a summer Saturday on the water near any home you are considering, and you will know quickly which kind of lake person you are.

## Fall is the reward

Fall brings cooler water, fewer boats and color along the shoreline. Many of my clients say fall is when the house finally feels like theirs. It is also a season when serious buyers are still looking and casual ones have stopped, which can suit a seller who prices well.

## Club life through the year

On the Gainesville side, the established communities around Harbour Point, Marina Bay and Chattahoochee Country Club give residents marinas, clubhouses and golf that carry on after the boating season ends. For buyers who want a social calendar in every month, these communities deserve a close look. Our [Gainesville](/gainesville/) page covers that shore in more detail.

## Buying for the whole year

When I help a client choose a lake home, I ask them to imagine every season in it. Where the morning sun lands in winter. Whether the road in is easy when it is wet. How the dock sits at low water. The answers point toward the right cove more reliably than any summer showing.
