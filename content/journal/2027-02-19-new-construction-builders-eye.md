Many buyers assume a new home needs less help than a resale. In my experience the opposite is often true. A new build involves a contract written by the builder, months of decisions and a dozen points where a small mistake becomes a permanent one. Before real estate I worked in construction management, and I still walk new construction at each stage with my clients.

## The lot comes first

The finished house will only be as good as the ground it sits on. I look at slope, drainage, how the lot sits against its neighbors and where the sun falls through the day. On larger lots in places like [Milton](/milton/) or the mountains, I also look at the well and septic plan and whether the site can support the outbuildings or pool a client may want later. Our [land and lots](/land-and-lots/) page covers more of what I look for.

## Read the builder's contract closely

Builder contracts are written to protect the builder. They set the terms for delays, changes, deposits and what counts as complete. I go through these with clients before they sign and make sure the questions are asked while there is still room to ask them. When the contract needs a legal opinion, we bring one in.

## Selections and upgrades

The design center is where budgets drift. I help clients separate the upgrades that are hard to add later, such as structural options, ceiling heights, windows and the electrical plan, from the finishes that are easy to change in a few years. My daughter Britney's renovation background helps here too, because she knows what a later change actually costs.

## The pre-drywall walk

This is the most important visit of the build. Once the walls close, the framing, plumbing, wiring and insulation disappear for good. I walk the house with the client before drywall and look for anything out of place. An independent inspector at this stage is money well spent, and I recommend one on every build.

## The final walk and the punch list

At the end, we go through the house room by room and write down everything that is not finished or not right. A good punch list is specific and dated. I stay involved until the items are closed, because a builder's attention moves to the next house quickly.

## The first year

Most builders offer a warranty period, and the issues that matter often appear after the first change of seasons. I encourage clients to keep a running list and to schedule a review before the warranty ends. I am glad to help with that conversation.

New construction can be a wonderful way to get exactly the home you want. It goes best with someone beside you who has stood on a job site.
