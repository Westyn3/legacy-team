Many of my clients reach a point where one home is not enough. They want a place in the mountains for weekends, holidays and eventually perhaps retirement. The two towns I am asked about most are Blue Ridge and Dahlonega. Both are lovely, and they suit different plans.

## Start with the drive

Distance decides how often you will actually use a second home. From Alpharetta, [Dahlonega](/dahlonega/) is about 50 minutes and [Blue Ridge](/blue-ridge/) is roughly 90. That difference sounds small and adds up quickly. A family that wants to leave on a Friday evening after work may find Dahlonega easier to use every week. A family planning longer stays may be happy to drive farther for Blue Ridge.

## Decide how you will use it

Blue Ridge has a busy, walkable downtown on the Scenic Railway, a clear mountain lake and the Toccoa River, with national forest all around. It suits owners who want trout streams, hiking and a town full of restaurants and galleries.

Dahlonega has a historic gold-rush square, the University of North Georgia and a ring of vineyards that have made it the center of Georgia's wine country. It suits owners who want tasting rooms, festivals and a town that stays active in every season. At the top of the market, Achasta offers a Jack Nicklaus signature golf course beside the Chestatee River.

## Be honest about renting

Many second-home owners rent their property when they are not using it. Blue Ridge has one of the most developed short-term rental markets in Georgia, and the Aska area along the Toccoa River draws the most interest from income-focused buyers. Dahlonega's year-round calendar also supports rental demand. Returns in either town vary widely with location, views, amenities and management, and some communities limit rentals through HOA rules or covenants. I review those limits before a client makes an offer.

If you plan to rent, decide early how much of the year you want the house for yourself. That answer shapes which property makes sense.

## Look beyond the town

Around Blue Ridge, Morganton, Mineral Bluff and Cherry Log offer more acreage and long views, often at better value. Around Dahlonega, cabins, farms and buildable lots cover the hills along the Chestatee and Etowah rivers. [Dawsonville](/dawsonville/) and Big Canoe are also worth a look for buyers who want mountain living a little closer to the lake.

## The property still matters most

Whichever town you choose, the same checks apply: the road in and its grade, well and septic, internet service and whether the view lasts all year. I go through each one before a client commits, and I wrote about that in more detail in an earlier post in our [journal](/journal/).
