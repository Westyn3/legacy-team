I worked in construction management before I sold real estate, and it changed how I walk a property. On an estate, the house is often the simplest part. Here is what I look at while there is still time to negotiate or walk away.

## Water and waste

Much of North Georgia outside city limits has no sewer. That means a well, a septic system, or both. I want to know that each is sized for the home as it stands today, including any additions, and I want to see the records.

## Everything that isn't the house

Pools, guest houses, barns and workshops each carry their own permits and their own maintenance. On equestrian property in [Milton](/milton/) I look at barn condition, fencing and how the pastures drain. A standard home inspection may not fully cover these, so we bring in the right specialists.

## The dock, if there is one

On [Lake Lanier](/lake-lanier/), the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers issues dock permits, and the permit belongs to the property. Some lots have one and some can never get one. I confirm the permit, the dock size allowed, and the water depth at full pool and at typical winter levels.

## The land itself

Slope decides what a foundation, a driveway and a retaining wall will cost. On a [lot you plan to build on](/land-and-lots/), that can run to six figures before the first footing is poured.

## Why it matters at this price

At the top of the market, a missed item is expensive. I would rather tell a client to pass on a beautiful property than watch them inherit a problem. If you are looking at [luxury homes](/luxury-homes/) anywhere from Milton to Blue Ridge, [call me](/contact/) before you write the offer.
