Luxury in the North Georgia mountains has become a market of its own. The homes at the top are defined by long-range views, high-end finishes and complete outdoor living spaces, and they draw buyers from Atlanta and from out of state. At this price, the house is often the easier part to judge. The mountain around it takes more work.

## Three places to start

In [Blue Ridge](/blue-ridge/), long-range-view homes and lakefront on Lake Blue Ridge begin around $1M and go well above it. The outlying communities of Morganton, Mineral Bluff and Cherry Log offer more acreage and long views. Downtown Atlanta is about an hour and 45 minutes away, and Alpharetta roughly 90 minutes.

In [Dahlonega](/dahlonega/), Achasta is the luxury address in Lumpkin County. It is a Jack Nicklaus signature golf community beside the Chestatee River, with a clubhouse and views of both the course and the mountains. Vineyard-view estates on the ridges near the tasting rooms offer more privacy. The town stays active through the year, which matters if you plan to live there or rent.

Big Canoe, in the [Dawsonville](/dawsonville/) area, is a gated mountain community that crosses from Dawson into Pickens County. It has lakes, golf, trails and a full set of amenities, and its homes are priced from the $500s up to seven figures.

## The view

A long-range view is most of what a buyer pays for here, so I test it. The first question is whether it holds all year or opens up only once the leaves fall. A view that looks endless in December can narrow considerably by summer. I also ask what could change it later, such as growth on land below the house that the owner does not control. Then I look at which way the house faces and how the decks and windows frame what you see.

## The slope and the site

Mountain lots are rarely flat. The grade decides where a house can sit, how the driveway climbs, and what an addition or new outdoor space would cost. On a property you plan to improve, I look at buildability before anything else. My construction management background is most useful at this stage, because a steep site can turn a modest project into a large one.

## The road in

Paved or gravel, the grade of the last stretch and how the road drives in winter all affect daily life and resale. I drive the road myself and ask who maintains it. A buyer who chooses a house in the fall should know exactly how they will reach it on a cold morning a few months later.

## The systems

Most mountain homes run on a well and a septic system. Each should have the capacity for the house in its current form and for the number of people it will hold, especially if it will be rented. I check internet availability, which matters to anyone who plans to work from the house. Decks and exterior wood take the weather hard, and their condition belongs on the list.

If rental income is part of the plan, I review any HOA or covenant limits on rentals and help build a realistic projection. Returns vary widely with location, views, amenities and management, and I would rather a buyer plan on a sober number.
