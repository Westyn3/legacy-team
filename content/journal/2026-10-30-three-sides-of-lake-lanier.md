Lake Lanier covers thirty-eight thousand acres and close to 700 miles of shoreline, yet buyers at the top of the market often talk about it as one place. I think of it as three. The Gainesville side, the south end around Buford and Flowery Branch, and the north end along the Dawsonville and Cumming shores each suit a different kind of owner. I have lived on the lake for more than twelve years, and this is how I explain the differences when a client's budget runs to seven figures.

## The Gainesville side

This is the established shore. Harbour Point is gated, with a yacht club, a marina, a pool and tennis. Marina Bay is gated as well, with a resort-style marina and a clubhouse near Lake Lanier Olympic Park, the rowing venue for the 1996 Games. At the Chattahoochee Country Club, a private golf course runs beside the water, and homes there seldom reach the market.

The city behind this shore sets it apart. [Gainesville](/gainesville/) is the seat of Hall County, with Northeast Georgia Medical Center, Brenau University and a downtown square full of restaurants and shops. A hospital, a grocery store and dinner on the square can all sit within ten minutes of the dock. Gated deep-water estates here run well into seven figures.

Who it suits: the owner who plans to live on the lake every month of the year and wants club life, a marina and city services close by. It also suits anyone who would rather join an established community than wait for a new one to mature.

## The south end

[Buford](/buford/) and [Flowery Branch](/flowery-branch/) sit at the end of the lake nearest Atlanta. Downtown is about 40 minutes from Buford and roughly 50 from Flowery Branch in normal traffic, and I-985 serves both. Lake Lanier Islands, Holiday Marina and Buford Dam mark this end of the water. In Flowery Branch, the Aqualand Marina area surrounds one of the largest marinas on the lake.

Schools carry real weight here. Buford runs its own city district, and only addresses inside the city limits are zoned for it, so I confirm the zoning before we look at a single cove. Lakefront in Buford begins around $700K and climbs well past $2M.

Who it suits: the family that still commutes, has children in school, or wants the shortest drive back to the city. The south end rewards a lake life lived on weekdays.

## The north end

Toward [Dawsonville](/dawsonville/) and the Cumming shores, the water runs deeper and quieter, the lots are larger and the views are longer. This end is less developed than the south, and it offers the best value per foot of shoreline on Lanier. Chestatee pairs a golf course with the lake, a marina and a clubhouse. On the Forsyth side, [Cumming](/cumming/) brings Forsyth County schools and lake neighborhoods near Bald Ridge Marina and Mary Alice Park. Deep-water lakefront on north Lanier begins in the high $800s, and estates on the Cumming shore run well past $3M.

Who it suits: the buyer who puts privacy, depth and room ahead of a short drive. Alpharetta is about 45 minutes from Dawsonville on GA-400, which keeps the north end within reach of the corridor.

## How I narrow it down

I start every lake search by asking what you want to do on the water. A boater who wants to be out on a Tuesday evening needs a different shore from a family that drives up on Fridays. After that come the commute, the schools and how much community you want around you. By the time we discuss bedrooms, the lake has usually chosen a side.
