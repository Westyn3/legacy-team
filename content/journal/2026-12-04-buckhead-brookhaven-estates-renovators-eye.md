Intown Atlanta has an estate market of its own, and it moves differently from the suburbs. The houses are often older, the streets carry decades of history, and a great address can come with several rounds of other people's renovations. I spent my first years in this business flipping and renovating homes. On an older estate, that is the lens I bring to every showing.

## The estate streets of Buckhead

Tuxedo Park and West Paces Ferry sit at the top of the Atlanta market. These are the streets of historic architecture behind gated drives, and the city's most significant homes change hands here. Prices run well past the $800K to $3M range where most of [Buckhead](/buckhead/)'s classic single-family homes trade. The Atlanta History Center and the shops and restaurants of Buckhead Village are close by.

To the north, the streets around Chastain Park suit a buyer who wants the city's largest park nearby, with its amphitheater, golf course, horse park and tennis center. Homes on those streets tend to sell fast, so preparation matters before one comes up.

## Historic Brookhaven

On the western side of [Brookhaven](/brookhaven/), Historic Brookhaven surrounds the golf course of the Capital City Club. It remains one of the most elegant and lasting addresses in Atlanta. Its estates trade above the $1.2M to $2M where much of the city's new construction lands. Many buyers at this level end up choosing between an estate here and one in Buckhead, and we tour both so the comparison is real.

## What I look for in an older estate

An older house on a great street can be the best buy on the block or the most expensive project in the neighborhood. A few things tell me which.

The first is the quality of past work. Many estates have been updated more than once. I look at how each phase was done and whether additions sit comfortably with the original house. Careful work from years ago can hold up well. Rushed work tends to show at the seams, around windows, rooflines and floors.

The second is what the next owner will want to change. Kitchens, baths and primary suites are often where an older estate shows its age. I give buyers an honest sense of scope before they settle on a number in their heads.

The third is the systems. Behind the finishes are the mechanical systems, the plumbing, the electrical and the roof. My mom, Karen, has a construction management background, and on a large or complicated house she reviews these with me. We would much prefer to find a problem during due diligence than after you move in.

The fourth is the lot. Buckhead's estate streets sit under hundred-year oaks, and mature trees are part of what a buyer pays for. They also need care, and their condition belongs in the conversation along with the slope, the drainage and the driveway.

## Pricing the difference

In both neighborhoods, value moves street by street and across school lines. Two listings that look alike can differ sharply once you account for the block or the condition. I tell clients plainly what a house is and what it would take to make it theirs, and we treat inspections as essential. That honesty matters as much to a seller pricing an estate as to a buyer weighing one.
