Spring is when many families decide to move up, move to the lake or move closer to the city. Most of them have a house to sell and a house to buy, and the hardest part is making the two fit together. With two of us working every file, we are well placed to manage both sides.

## Decide which comes first

There are three basic approaches. Sell first and rent or stay with family while you buy. Buy first and carry two homes for a while. Or try to close both on the same day or the same week. Each has a cost. Selling first is the safest financially and the least convenient. Buying first is the most comfortable and the most expensive. A same-week closing balances the two and requires careful planning.

We help you choose based on your finances, your lender's guidance and how much uncertainty you can live with.

## Talk to the lender early

Your lender will tell you what you can afford with and without the sale of your current home. That answer shapes everything else, including whether you can make an offer without a sale contingency. We introduce clients to lenders we trust and make sure everyone is working from the same numbers.

## Prepare the sale before you shop

The more ready your current home is, the more control you have. Karen walks the house with a builder's eye and flags anything an inspector is likely to find. Britney decides which updates will pay for themselves and plans the photography and marketing. If the home can go to market quickly, you can write a stronger offer on the next one. Our [sell](/sell/) page covers that preparation in detail.

## Write the purchase with the sale in mind

When you find the right house, the offer needs terms that protect you. That might mean a sale contingency, a longer closing date, a rent-back from the seller or a rent-back to your own buyer. We know which of these sellers tend to accept and which weaken an offer. Our [buying guide](/buy/) explains the main terms.

## One team watching both timelines

This is where a team of two pays off. One of us usually leads the sale while the other leads the purchase, and we talk about both every day. If an inspection on your sale raises a repair request, the person leading your purchase already knows and can adjust that timeline before it becomes a problem.

## Moving across regions

Many of our clients sell in one part of North Georgia and buy in another, for example a family home in [Alpharetta](/alpharetta/) and a deep-water home on [Lake Lanier](/lake-lanier/). Because we divide the map between us, you have the right local expert on each side and one team holding it all together.

## Moving day

We coordinate closing dates, key handoffs and the final walk-throughs so you are not caught between two homes. When the boxes are unpacked, we are still a phone call away.
