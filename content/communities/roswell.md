Roswell began around a mill on Vickery Creek in 1839, which makes it one of the oldest cities in North Fulton. The historic district still sets the tone. Canton Street has become a favorite evening out across metro Atlanta, a walkable run of chef-led restaurants and galleries with Bulloch Hall and the mill ruins nearby. The river is the second half of the story. Roswell holds miles of Chattahoochee frontage, and the Riverwalk, the parks and the Chattahoochee Nature Center give residents an outdoor routine that few suburbs can offer.

The housing is mostly established. Swim-tennis neighborhoods sit under mature trees, and mid-century ranches near the historic district earn a premium once renovated. Townhome communities have gone up within walking distance of Canton Street. At the upper end, we look to riverfront and river-view homes and to country club living at Horseshoe Bend and Willow Springs. Roswell High and Centennial High are both well regarded. People tend to move here deliberately and stay a long time, which keeps listings scarce in the areas buyers want most.

Britney leads our Roswell work. Buyers here usually fall for the setting first, and her renovation background helps make sure the house itself deserves the same confidence, particularly with older homes near the historic core.
