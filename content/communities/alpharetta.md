Alpharetta was once a quiet Fulton County farm town. Today it holds one of the Southeast's largest clusters of technology companies, hundreds of them along GA-400, which is how it came to be called the Technology City of the South. The growth did not cost the city its center. Main Street has been revived with restaurants, boutiques and events on the City Green, and Avalon, a few minutes east, combines luxury retail, dining, offices and residences in a single walkable district. The same highway runs south to Buckhead and north to Cumming and Lake Lanier.

Demand here is deep and largely professional, and the housing is more varied than first-time visitors expect. There are condos and modern townhomes within walking distance of downtown or Avalon, wooded swim-tennis neighborhoods, lake and golf living in Windward, and new luxury construction across the city. Along the northern edge, estate homes share Milton's schools. Daily life includes the Big Creek Greenway trails, the recreation campus at Wills Park and summer concerts at Ameris Bank Amphitheatre. Alpharetta High and Cambridge High are among the schools that draw families.
