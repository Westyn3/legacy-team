Norcross is an authentic railroad town from the 1870s, and its downtown was never torn down or rebuilt to look like something else. Along South Peachtree Street, brick storefronts hold restaurants, shops and taprooms. Lillian Webb Park fills its lawn for concerts and movie nights. The historic district around it, with Victorian and craftsman houses, is the top of the local market and gives Norcross a character that newer suburbs can only approximate. Britney leads our work here.

Outside the core lies some of the most attainable housing in the inner metro: established ranch and split-level neighborhoods, townhome communities, and steady renovation. Many buyers arrive after being priced out of Brookhaven, Chamblee or Peachtree Corners. New construction is active too, though not as large master-planned subdivisions. It takes the form of townhomes and infill builds near downtown and toward the Peachtree Corners edge. Britney walks those builds with clients from the first site visit through the final punch list.

The location serves owners well. Peachtree Industrial and I-85 bring the job centers of Gwinnett and the Perimeter within easy reach, and the tech corridor of Peachtree Corners is next door. International restaurants along Buford Highway and Jimmy Carter Boulevard surround the city. Norcross is still growing outward from its historic center, which is both its limitation and its opportunity. We help clients judge which streets are furthest along, drawing on Britney's experience renovating homes.
