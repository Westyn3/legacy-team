For many families, Lake Lanier is the reason for moving to North Georgia at all. It lies less than an hour from Atlanta and reaches into Hall, Forsyth, Gwinnett, Dawson and Lumpkin counties. Its communities vary widely, from long-established golf and marina neighborhoods in Gainesville to quiet deep-water coves near Dawsonville. Few markets in the state reward local knowledge as much as this one. Karen's first question to a lake buyer is how they intend to use the water, because the answer decides which side of the lake to start on.

A lake purchase works differently from a house on a street. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers manages the shoreline. A dock permit therefore belongs to the property and not to its owner, and some lakefront lots do not qualify for one. Depth is the other variable. It shifts with the lake level and the time of year, so a cove with good water in May may be a mudflat by a dry October. We go through these points with every lake buyer before an offer is written.

Selling on the lake is a discipline of its own. Whether the property is a year-round residence, a weekend place or a home one family has held for decades, the approach combines lake knowledge with luxury marketing. Karen has both, along with a construction-management background and more than a decade living on Lanier. Our team also reaches further than the shoreline. Britney works North Atlanta and intown, so a client moving between a city house and a lake estate deals with one team from start to finish.
