America's first major gold rush happened in Dahlonega, and the public square still reflects it. The old courthouse stands among restaurants and shops, and a full calendar of festivals brings visitors throughout the year. The University of North Georgia adds steady activity and a base of renters. In the Lumpkin County hills around town, tasting rooms and vineyard estates line the ridges, and the area has become the center of Georgia's wine country.

Because of that mix, the market runs all year instead of by season. Our buyers here include retirees, second-home owners, remote workers and families who want mountain living alongside a town that functions. At the top of the market is Achasta, a Jack Nicklaus signature golf community on the Chestatee River. Beyond it, the countryside offers cabins, farms and view lots. As Karen puts it, this is a mountain town that stays open in January, which matters to full-time residents and to owners who rent.

Karen leads our Dahlonega work. The town marks the northern edge of her core territory, roughly 30 minutes from Lake Lanier's north shore and 45 from Cumming on GA-400. She assesses mountain property on its own terms: access roads, well and septic, slope, and whether a view survives once the leaves return. For sellers, we reach the Atlanta and out-of-state buyers who search here, helped by Britney's presence across North Atlanta.
