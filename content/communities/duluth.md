When planners in metro Atlanta discuss suburban downtowns done well, Duluth is usually the first one named. The Town Green and Parsons Alley turned the historic center into a destination. Restaurants and dessert shops occupy restored buildings, the green has an amphitheater and a splash fountain, and festivals run through most of the year. Few places in Gwinnett have a center that residents visit weekly. Britney leads our Duluth work.

The range of housing is broad. At the top is Sugarloaf Country Club, a gated community of estate homes on a TPC course that hosts nationally significant golf. Established swim-tennis neighborhoods feed well-regarded schools such as Peachtree Ridge and Duluth High. Near downtown, townhomes and new construction bring life by the Town Green within reach of first-time buyers. Duluth is also among the most international cities in the metro, known across the Southeast for Korean dining and retail.

Buyers get real amenities and a real community at Gwinnett prices, with I-85, Peachtree Industrial and the events of the Gas South District close by.
