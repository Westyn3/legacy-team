Gainesville is the seat of Hall County and the biggest city on Lake Lanier. Those two facts give it a downtown, employers and infrastructure that smaller lake towns do without. Restaurants and shops fill the revitalized square. Northeast Georgia Medical Center anchors one of the largest employers in the region, and Brenau University lends the feel of a college town. People live here all twelve months, which matters to buyers who want more than summer weekends.

Along the shoreline are the most established communities on the lake, among them Harbour Point, Marina Bay and the Chattahoochee Country Club. Lake Lanier Olympic Park, the rowing venue for the 1996 Games, remains one of the better public access points on the water. For buyers who want a hospital, a grocery store and a downtown within ten minutes of the dock, Gainesville is usually the first place we look, and often the last.

The housing stock is broad. Near the square are historic homes, and newer neighborhoods follow the Thompson Bridge corridor. Cresswind at Lake Lanier, a 55+ community, attracts active retirees from around the country. On the water, the choices run from lake-access ranches up to gated deep-water estates. Karen leads our Gainesville work and has lived on this side of the lake for years. She knows which neighborhoods hold their value and which remain quietly underpriced.
