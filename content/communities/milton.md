Milton became a city in 2006, taking its name from Revolutionary War figure John Milton, and it was formed to protect something specific: the pastures, horse farms and two-lane wooded roads of this part of North Fulton. Large-lot zoning across much of the city has kept that intact. Estates on acreage, equestrian farms and gated golf communities shape the market here instead of dense subdivisions, and supply stays limited as a result. Owners can keep horses on their own land and still reach downtown Alpharetta, Avalon and GA-400 within about fifteen minutes.

Crabapple gives the city a center. The district has restaurants, shops, the green at City Hall and community evenings such as food truck nights. Around it, the housing covers considerable ground: cottage-style homes and townhomes near the village, swim-tennis neighborhoods, club communities including The Manor, White Columns and Crooked Creek, and the horse country along Birmingham Highway, Freemanville Road and Hopewell Road. Those farms are among the most distinctive properties in the metro area. Demand holds at each price level, which helps explain why values here have been durable.
